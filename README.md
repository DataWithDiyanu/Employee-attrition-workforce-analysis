# Employee Attrition & Workforce Analysis

## Project Overview

For this project, I worked with a synthetic employee dataset containing 2,500 employee records to explore employee attrition and understand some of the workforce patterns within the organisation.

I wanted to go beyond simply counting the number of employees who left. I wanted to look at different employee groups and identify patterns that could help explain where attrition was relatively high and which areas might need further attention.

I used Google Sheets to inspect and clean the data, perform my analysis, create pivot tables and visualisations, and build a dashboard to present my findings.

## Project Objectives

The main objectives of this project were to:

- Assess the overall employee attrition and retention rates.
- Identify departments and job roles with relatively high attrition.
- Examine how factors such as job satisfaction, overtime, tenure, salary and employment type relate to employee attrition.
- Identify workforce groups that may require further investigation.
- Present the findings through a dashboard and make practical recommendations.

## Tools Used

- Google Sheets
- Spreadsheet formulas and functions
- Pivot tables

## What I Did

I started by inspecting the original dataset and checking the quality of the information.

I then:
- Identified and reviewed missing values.
- Checked for duplicate employee IDs and inconsistent entries.
- Standardised inconsistent categorical values.
- Created helper columns to group employee tenure, salary and commute time.
- Calculated the overall attrition and retention rates.
- Used pivot tables to compare attrition across different workforce categories.
- Created charts to make the results easier to understand.
- Built a dashboard to bring the key findings together.

## Key Findings

From my analysis of the 2,500 employee records, I found the following:

- **Overall Attrition:** 392 employees had left, representing an attrition rate of 15.68%. The remaining 2,108 employees represented a retention rate of 84.32%.
- **Job Satisfaction:** Employees with low job satisfaction recorded the highest attrition rate at 26.28%, compared with 14.60% for medium satisfaction and 10.94% for high satisfaction.
- **Overtime:** Employees who worked overtime had an attrition rate of 22.11%, compared with 12.12% among those who did not.
- **Employee Tenure:** Employees with 0–2 years of service recorded the highest number of departures, with 180 employees leaving.
- **Department:** IT recorded the highest departmental attrition rate at 19.89%.
- **Job Role:** Data Analysts recorded the highest job-role attrition rate at 23.42%.
- **Employment Type:** Contract employees had the highest attrition rate at 20.10%.
- **Promotion:** Employees who had not been promoted in the previous two years recorded a higher attrition rate (17.34%) than those who had (9.65%).

## Recommendations

Based on the patterns I identified, I would recommend that management investigate the following areas:

**1. Employee Satisfaction**

Since employees with low job satisfaction recorded the highest attrition rate, management should try to understand what is contributing to this. This could include conducting employee satisfaction surveys, having regular feedback sessions and reviewing factors such as management support, recognition, workload, working conditions and opportunities for growth.

**2. Overtime and Workload Management**

The attrition rate was higher among employees who worked overtime. Management should investigate why overtime is necessary and whether it is becoming a regular part of employees' schedules.

This could involve reviewing staffing levels, workload distribution, delivery targets, scheduling practices and operational bottlenecks. Where overtime is frequent, management should consider whether additional staffing or better work allocation could reduce the pressure on employees. Overtime compensation, adequate rest and work-life balance should also be reviewed.

**3. Support for New Employees**

Employees with 0–2 years of service accounted for the highest number of departures. Management should pay closer attention to the early stages of employment by improving onboarding, providing adequate training, assigning mentors and scheduling regular check-ins to understand the challenges new employees may be experiencing.

**4. Career Development and Promotion Opportunities**

The analysis showed a higher attrition rate among employees who had not been promoted in the previous two years. Management should review career development opportunities, make promotion criteria clearer and ensure employees understand the skills and expectations required for career progression.

**5. Further Investigation of High-Attrition Departments and Roles**

IT had the highest departmental attrition rate, while Data Analysts had the highest job-role attrition rate. Management should investigate these areas more closely by reviewing workload, job expectations, management support, employee development and working conditions.

These recommendations are areas for investigation and possible action. The analysis identifies patterns and associations; it does not establish the actual causes of employee departures.
